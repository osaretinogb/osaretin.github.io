Environmental analytics · Time-series forecasting

Forecasting criteria air contaminants

How might reported industrial air releases change over five years, and where should an environmental analyst look more closely?

From reporting data to a forecast

I worked with Canada’s National Pollutant Release Inventory (NPRI) to isolate air releases, check units and reconcile facility names. I compared regression models for seven pollutant categories, using earlier emissions as inputs, then used each year’s prediction to forecast the next.

Results & how I shared them

The exploratory notebook charts historical releases by pollutant, province and industry. The forecasting notebook compares models and summarizes their predictions at those same levels so an analyst can see where reported releases may be concentrated.

What I’d improve

Feeding predictions back into the model can compound errors, and the forecast starts with facilities reporting in the latest available year for each pollutant. I’d backtest each forecast year against a last-observed-value baseline and show uncertainty ranges before using the results for planning.

Analytics · Independent project

Cleaning Nashville housing data

Property data is harder to analyse when dates, addresses and vacancy values are inconsistent and the same sale may appear more than once.

My approach

I wrote a SQL Server script that standardizes sale dates, fills missing property addresses using matching parcel IDs, splits address fields, and uses ROW_NUMBER() to remove repeated sale records.

Result & business value

The script creates consistent fields for grouping sales by date or city. It also puts vacancy values into a consistent format so reports can count those records together.

Data quality & reflection

A parcel can have more than one address in the source. On a production dataset I’d flag conflicting matches for review, log the rows I changed, and compare row counts before and after duplicate removal.

Predictive analytics · Team project

Automotive service retention

Can a service team identify customers who may skip their next visit so it can prioritize reminders or loyalty offers?

From a business question to a model

I helped turn the retention use case into a measurable question by using service history to see whether a vehicle had a later visit. I explored patterns in mileage, service history and loyalty-card use and compared different classification models with the team.

What the results showed

In the team’s evaluation that kept the same vehicles from appearing in both training and testing, Random Forest reached 83% overall accuracy. It identified only about 52% of the non-return cases, the group the service team most wanted to reach.

How I’d use the result

I’d define a clear return window and test on newer visits so recent customers have time to return. I’d choose a cutoff based on how many missed customers the service team can tolerate and how many reminders it can send, then check whether outreach actually helps.

Data analytics · SQL & Tableau preparation

COVID-19 data exploration

How can a reporting analyst compare recorded cases and deaths across countries with different population sizes?

My approach

I wrote SQL Server queries to compare reported cases and deaths by location and date. I used population to put case totals in context and joined case and vaccination records by location and date to calculate a running total of vaccination doses.

Reporting output

I prepared a separate set of queries for Tableau, with inputs for global totals, deaths by region, and reported cases relative to population. The location and date query makes it possible to explore how those rates changed over time.

What I’d improve

Testing and reporting practices differ across locations. I’d show reporting coverage and dates, check for overlapping geographic totals, and label cumulative doses separately from the number of people vaccinated.

Personal product · AI-assisted planning

MyG Accountability App

I built MyG for people who have a goal in mind and need help figuring out what to do today and staying accountable as they work on it.

What I built

I built a Streamlit app that helps users turn a goal into a SMART goal and generates a plan they can review and revise. The app uses Supabase for sign-in and saving plans. Users can check in on their steps, choose which plan to share with an accountability partner, and receive comments on shared steps.

How I’m testing the idea

The app includes a feedback link so testers can report where the planning process gets confusing. I’d watch how quickly someone gets from entering a goal to completing their first step and use that feedback to simplify setup.

Privacy & AI safeguards

The app gives users a chance to review AI-generated steps and lets them choose what to share with partners. The beta asks users to avoid entering highly sensitive details. Before widening access, I’d test that partners can only see the plans actually shared with them.
